# OpenOrgOS Branding Guidelines

These guidelines outline the general rules for anyone who wants to use **OpenOrgOS** and **OpenOrgOS Community** brand assets. Consistent use helps people identify references to the project and protects our marks. Any use must comply with our [Trademark Usage Guidelines](/legal/trademark-usage) in addition to the visual rules on this page.

## Our name

**OpenOrgOS** refers to the global inter-organizational protocol. **OpenOrgOS Community** refers to the open-source community that develops modules, standards, and governance around that protocol.

Do not imply that OpenOrgOS is an internal HR system, ERP replacement, or legal authority. The global layer defines protocol mechanics only; local business logic lives in committees.

## Logo usage

When a logo mark is provided:

- Use the **primary stacked** version where possible.
- Use the **horizontal** version only when space is constrained.
- On dark backgrounds, use the **white** variation.
- Maintain clear space around the mark — at least the height of the “O” in OpenOrgOS.

Do not stretch, rotate, recolor outside the palette, or combine our logo into third-party logos.

## Colors

| Role | Value | Usage |
|------|-------|--------|
| Navy | `#0B2545` | Headers, footer, primary UI |
| Accent | `#1F6FEB` | Links, CTAs |
| Muted | `#8B9CB3` | Secondary text on dark backgrounds |
| Surface | `#F6F8FB` | Page backgrounds |

## Acceptable use

### Community & education

You may reference OpenOrgOS in docs, talks, and course materials when the context is accurate. Example: “Built on modules from the OpenOrgOS Community.”

### Membership & affiliation

It is acceptable to say **“Contributor to OpenOrgOS Community”** or **“Maintainer of [module name]”** when true. Link to your public profile or module page when possible.

### Commercial use

Check with the community before using our marks on products, packaging, websites, or advertising. Your name and logo must be more prominent than OpenOrgOS marks.

## Please do not

- Use “OpenOrgOS” in your company, product, or domain name in a confusing way.
- Edit or remix official logos.
- Suggest sponsorship, endorsement, or certification we have not granted.
- Use marks on merchandise without approval.

## Attribution

When using our name in materials, include a notice such as:

> OpenOrgOS and OpenOrgOS Community are community projects. This [product / publication / event] is not affiliated with or sponsored by OpenOrgOS unless explicitly stated.
