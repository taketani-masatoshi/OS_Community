OpenOrgOS is an **organizational EHR** — an open-source community building the global protocol for inter-organizational communication.

When care teams collaborate, they share one patient chart: history, responsibility, delegation, and an audit trail. Companies, universities, NPOs, and supply chains need the same pattern at organizational scale — **verifiable shared records** across boundaries. OpenOrgOS defines that protocol layer.

> **It is not an internal HR or payroll OS.** It does not replace intranet workflows. It is the common language for relationships where organizations exchange state across boundaries.

## Why an organizational EHR?

| Medical EHR | OpenOrgOS |
| --- | --- |
| Shared chart | Structured record of org-to-org interactions |
| Chart entries | **Org Event Model** — contracts, delegations, state changes |
| Patient ID | **Identity Exchange** — who organizations and actors are |
| Referrals & handoffs | **Authority Delegation** — scoped powers and agency |
| Audit trail | **Auditability** — timelines third parties can verify |

## What the global protocol defines

| Layer | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Org Event Model** | Common model for events exchanged between organizations |
| **Identity Exchange** | Exchange of organizational and actor identity |
| **Authority Delegation** | Standardized delegation of powers, scopes, and agency |
| **Auditability** | Third-party verifiability of what happened and when |

## Delegated to committees

**Business logic, legal interpretation, and organizational behavior live in national or domain-specific committees.**

The global layer defines the protocol only. Accounting, contracts, labor rules, and industry regulation are implemented by **national or domain-specific committees**.

## Human-readable rules

Rules are written in **Markdown, YAML**, and other human-readable formats.

- AI processes the rules
- People maintain the rules
- The community grows an auditable, trustworthy protocol

Modules implement the protocol for jurisdictions, industries, and use cases. They are **not** meant to replace internal HR or payroll systems.

## What Open means

**Open** means incorporating the power and opinions of everyone who uses the protocol.

Proposals, reviews, standardization, and module development — an open-source community anyone can join.

## Scope

Trade between companies, university partnerships, NPO alliances, supply chains, joint ventures — any relationship where organizations must exchange **verifiable state across boundaries**.

## Membership

**Contributors and reviewers are welcome regardless of specialty or region.**

Participation in committees **outside your own specialty or region** follows stricter criteria. For example, joining a China-focused committee as a Japanese participant typically requires deep expertise in the same domain (accounting, contracts, etc.) and the ability to bridge rules across jurisdictions.

See [Governance](/governance) for details.

## Openness and protecting competitive assets

OpenOrgOS does **not** standardize competitive business domains. What is shared is the **exchange protocol**; products, customers, know-how, and strategy remain **protected by each organization**.

You may combine **Community Modules (public)** and **Private Modules (non-public)** freely. Contributing back to the community is welcome but not required.

See the [Openness and competitive asset protection policy](/governance/openness) for details.
